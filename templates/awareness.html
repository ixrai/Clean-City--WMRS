Household & Community Waste Segregation Guide

Segregating waste at source is the single most effective intervention to prevent landfill fires, protect groundwater from toxic leachate, and support municipal sanitation workers.

Green Bin
Wet / Biodegradable Waste

Organic material that decomposes naturally into nutrient-rich compost.

Included:
  • Vegetable & fruit peels
  • Cooked leftover food
  • Eggshells, tea bags, coffee grounds
  • Fallen leaves, garden cuttings
Do NOT include:
  • Plastic carry bags
  • Milk pouches
Blue Bin
Dry / Recyclable Waste

Inorganic clean items that can be processed and remanufactured.

Included:
  • Cardboard boxes & paper
  • PET plastic bottles, milk pouches
  • Aluminum cans, tin containers
  • Glass jars and bottles
Do NOT include:
  • Soiled pizza boxes
  • Food-contaminated wrappers
Red Bin
Domestic Hazardous Waste

Items containing toxic, corrosive, or flammable components.

Included:
  • Expired pharmaceutical drugs
  • Syringes, bandages, clinical gloves
  • Insecticide sprays & paint tins
  • Bleach, drain cleaners
Precaution:
  • Wrap sharps in newspaper before disposal
Black Bin
Electronic & Special Waste

Electrical appliances and components containing precious and toxic heavy metals.

Included:
  • Old smartphones, broken remotes
  • Lithium-ion & dry-cell batteries
  • USB cables, chargers, adapters
  • Fluorescent tubes and CFL lamps
Best Practice:
  • Hand over to authorized e-waste recyclers

The 5 R's of Urban Sustainability

1
Refuse
Say no to single-use plastics and packaging.
2
Reduce
Buy only what is needed; eliminate excess waste.
3
Reuse
Repurpose containers, bags, and jars.
4
Repurpose
Convert waste into planters or home utilities.
5
Recycle
Route sorted dry waste to authorized recycling facilities.
